# Killing the process while an LED is lit

**Date:** 2026-10-02. **Software:** 0.1.0.dev0 (commit `d052a24`). **Device:** one HC1500
(`hc1500-1`) in a dark box, white paper in view. Only LED 6 (520 nm) and LED 9 (630 nm)
were lit. A second process watched the camera and printed the mean of the picture twice
a second; it sent nothing to the light source.

| Step | What the camera showed |
|---|---|
| `vispek-hc led on 6 224` | lit about 0.9 s later (mean green 70.9) |
| `kill -9` of that process | **still lit**, unchanged for the 6 s until the next step |
| `vispek-hc led off --all` | dark (5, 5, 5) within 1 s; the command reported `all_off: true` |
| `vispek-hc led on 9 144`, then `kill -TERM` | dark within 0.5 s |
| `vispek-hc led on 6 224` started by a script as a background job, then `kill -INT` | **still lit**: nothing happened |
| `kill -TERM` of that process | dark within 1 s |

## Conclusions

1. A process that is killed outright leaves its LED lit, as [SAFETY.md](../../SAFETY.md)
   says. The device does not switch it off by itself within 6 s. `vispek-hc led off --all`
   from a new process puts it out and reports it.
2. SIGTERM switches the LED off before the process ends.
3. A process that a shell script started in the background does not react to SIGINT:
   such a shell starts its background jobs with SIGINT ignored, and the software leaves
   an ignored signal alone. Ctrl-C in a terminal is not affected. End a background
   process with SIGTERM.
